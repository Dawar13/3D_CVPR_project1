Do 3D models trust bad hints? Where we stand, and what comes next

What has been found, how the experiments are set up, what the planned fix does, why bad hints matter in the real world, and how the next GPU sessions decide the final paper.

The whole paper in three sentences

  1. The problem. Programs that build 3D models from photos accept extra hints (where each camera was, lens settings, depth maps) and obey them, even when they are wrong, and even when the program itself can tell.
  2. It matters. Real hint sources (AI depth estimates, phone compasses, GPS, long phone recordings) make errors of the size that hurts.
  3. A fix. A cheap check that compares the hints with what the photos alone say can repair or drop bad hints without retraining, and leave good hints alone.

1. The question

Programs like MapAnything turn a handful of photos into a 3D model. They can also take hints: where each camera was (position and direction), lens settings, and depth maps. Correct hints make them much better. Real hints come from phones, GPS, drones or AI depth estimators, and these are sometimes wrong. We ask three things:

  1. Do wrong hints make the result worse than using no hints at all?
  2. Why does it happen?
  3. Can we catch and fix bad hints automatically, using only the photos, with no retraining?

3.3 How a test works

Each clip is 10 photos plus hints. Each program runs several ways on the same clip:

  1. Photos only, no hints.
  2. Correct hints, the best case.
  3. Wrong hints: errors put in on purpose (ETH3D) or real iPad / AI hints (SceneFun3D).
  4. Wrong hints plus a remedy: the existing remedies (3.4), and later our fix.

Errors are compared clip by clip, with the room or scene as the unit, so ten clips of one room cannot count as ten pieces of evidence. With many comparisons a statistical correction is applied, and an effect only counts if it is also big enough to matter: 1 percentage point for shape, 0.25° for direction, 5% for size.

What is measured: shape error of the 3D points; camera-direction error between camera pairs and the share of pairs within 5°; the standard pose score other papers report (AUC@30, now computed for 11,938 finished runs); depth error; real-world size error. For the fix also: how much damage it repairs, how often it wrongly touches good hints, and the extra running time.

Source: results/e2c_eval/auc30.csv; results/e2df_eval/auc30.csv; results/e2d_eval/auc30.csv; results/e2e_ma_eval/auc30.csv; results/e2e_ov_eval/auc30.csv

Our answer to "isn't this rare?"

  1. Not for AI depth. Every iPad clip had a scale error between 13% and 85%, and it hurt both programs.
  2. For camera directions it depends on the source, and we say so openly: phone tracking over a short walk is good (it helped), while compass-based directions, GPS and long recordings are not.
  3. The fix is a seatbelt. If it costs almost nothing on good hints and never makes them worse, it is worth having even when failures are uncommon. So the paper must show, and report, how rarely it touches good hints.

Honest gap: the only real-world harm we have measured ourselves so far is AI depth. Measured real-world harm from wrong camera directions depends on Part X-B (whole-room iPad clips).

5. The fix (E3), explained step by step

5.1 Why does the fix run "photos only", if that is a baseline?

The photos-only run has two different jobs, and only one of them is the baseline:

The reason the fix needs it: no fixed choice wins everywhere. With good hints, using them is much better (MapAnything's own paper, two-photo benchmark: relative point error 0.08 with photos only versus 0.02 with all hints). With bad hints, ignoring them is better (E1). A real system does not know which case it is in. So the fix decides clip by clip and hint by hint, and the photos-only answer is the referee it uses to decide, not the final answer.

Use all hintsPhotos only (R0)The fix (goal)
Hints are goodbestclearly worseas good as using all hints
Hints are badmuch worse (E1, E2)safeas good as photos only, or better (it keeps the good hints)

5.2 The three ways hints go wrong

Picture 10 cameras along a walk. Each hint says which way a camera was pointing. Hints can be wrong in three typical ways:

KindPlain exampleWhat the photos-only answer shows
1. One shared tiltThe phone's compass is off by 5°, the camera is mounted slightly crooked, or the first camera's direction is wrong (every other camera is measured relative to it)Every hint differs from the photos by the same angle. Each one looks only a little off; together they are consistently off.
2. DriftTracking adds a tiny mistake at every step: camera 2 is 0.5° off, camera 3 1°, … camera 10 4.5°The difference grows steadily from camera to camera.
3. One bad cameraA GPS jump or a compass glitch next to metalOne camera disagrees strongly; the others agree.

5.3 What the fix does in each case

KindWhat the fix doesWhy this way
One shared tiltMeasures the common angle and turns all hints back by itThe hints are still correct relative to each other, so all of that information is kept
DriftMeasures how fast the error grows and undoes itSame: the rest of each hint is kept
One bad cameraDrops that one camera's hintIts hint has nothing worth keeping
Depth too far or too nearPredicts depth from the camera hints alone, compares, and rescales the depth hints (e.g. divides AI depth by about 1.4)AI depth is usually wrong in overall scale, not in shape

Worked example (shared tilt). The photos-only answer says camera 2 turned 10° and camera 3 turned 20° from camera 1. The hints say 15° and 25°. Every hint is 5° more than the photos, so the fix subtracts 5° from all hints. They now agree with the photos, and the final run uses all 10 corrected hints. E1's filter would have kept all of them unchanged, because each one is only 5° off, under its 10° limit, and the damage stays.

5.4 "Act only when the photos are sure"

The photos-only answer is not perfect either: typically 0.8° off (2.0° in the worst 10% of clips), and on some iPad trial clips 2–4.5° off overall, or 15° on a single camera, even when the hints were correct. If hints and photos disagree by 1°, we cannot tell who is wrong.

So the fix first measures how sure the photos are on this clip: it runs photos-only three times with the photos in a different order. If the three answers agree, the photos are a reliable witness here; if they wobble, they are not. A hint is repaired or dropped only if it disagrees with the photos by several times the photos' own wobble. Otherwise it is left alone.

Think of two witnesses. If one of them is unsure of what they saw, you do not use their story to overrule the other.

The open risk. On the iPad trial clips the photos themselves were sometimes unreliable (above). If the "how sure" measurement does not catch those cases, the fix would damage good hints. This is exactly what the calibration in the next GPU session checks first.

6. Extended E2: what the next GPU session does

Part X-A: is obeying common?

π3-X gets exactly the same broken ETH3D hints as MapAnything and OmniVGGT (identical down to the random numbers). Does it copy wrong hints, or overrule them as SpatialBench suggests? Either answer is informative.

Part X-B: do real hints hurt when the error is bigger?

All three programs on the whole-room iPad clips (π3-X also on the short clips): photos only, true hints, iPad poses, iPad depth sensor, AI depth, and remedies R2/R3. Do real iPad poses still help across a whole room? Does AI depth hurt again?

Part E3-calibration: setting the fix's cut-offs without peeking

  • The fix runs on calibration rooms (10 rooms never used for final results) and the ETH3D trial clips, with every kind of broken hint and with real hints. Every decision it makes is logged.
  • Rule, fixed in advance: choose the cut-off so that, on correct hints, the fix acts on at most 5% of clips (shared/drift repairs) and drops at most 2% of cameras. "Do no harm" comes first.
  • How well it catches errors is recorded but not used to choose the cut-off, so the choice cannot be tuned toward a nice-looking result.

Jobs run most important first, so an early stop only loses the least important ones. Each final run starts only if its trial run passes automatic checks. Estimate (not measured): about 2.5–3.5 GPU-hours; π3-X's speed is unknown until its trial run.

Source: experiments/E2X.md (sections 4-6); configs/e2x_*.json; scripts/session_queue_e2x.sh

7. How E3 gets decided

  1. After the GPU session (laptop): analyse X-A, X-B and the calibration.
  2. Lock the E3 plan before the final E3 run: the cut-offs from calibration, the final clips, the remedies to beat, and the success rules.
  3. Second GPU session: the fix against all remedies on the final clips: ETH3D final scenes with every kind of broken hint, whole-room iPad clips with real iPad hints, iPad clips with AI depth.

Proposed success rules (fixed in step 2, so not final yet):

  • Repairs damage: on errors shared by all cameras (compass, drift, first camera, depth scale), where the existing remedies leave at least half of the damage, the fix removes clearly more. The original target was about 70% of what the cheating ORACLE removes.
  • Does no harm: on correct hints and on good real iPad hints, any change stays below the "big enough to matter" thresholds.
  • Beats the best existing remedy on those error types.
