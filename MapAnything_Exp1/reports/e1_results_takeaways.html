What this means, in plain words

1. Wrong camera directions are the danger. One camera turned 15° wrong made camera-rotation error 114% worse than giving no poses at all, in all 10 scenes (30°: 321% worse). Every camera jittered by 2°: 52% worse (8/10 scenes). 20% of the cameras wrong: 295% worse (10/10). The first (reference) camera turned 15°: 3D shape 164% worse, rotation 124% worse.
2. Errors that agree with each other hurt, and the simple filter cannot see them. Odometry-style drift (level 2) made rotation 142% worse than no poses (10/10 scenes); the same 5° heading error on every camera, 108% worse (9/10). The "compare with photos-only" filter recovered about 10% of that loss at most. It fixes big single outliers (15° or more: 97-99% recovered), but not a 5° outlier (4%) and not drift or a shared heading error.
3. Wrong camera positions are mostly harmless. Position jitter and small glitches still left results 47-83% better than photos only. Only a far jump hurts the 3D shape: 3 scene-lengths, 62% worse in 8 of 10 scenes, but the 95% interval still touches zero, so it does not count as harm; 10 scene-lengths, 348% worse.
4. The model copies wrong scale. Telling it every distance is 1.5× too long made its scene 1.38× too big (95% CI 1.32-1.43); the real-size 3D error rose 75% above photos only (8/10 scenes). Depth hints 1.5× too deep on 2 of 10 views made the 3D shape 139% worse than without depth hints (10/10); one such view alone, 78% worse. Slightly noisy depth (5%) did no harm.
5. Wrong lens settings hurt only a little. A focal length 10% too long on every photo made rotation 18% worse than no lens info (+0.05°, 8/10 scenes), and the run was still 66% better than dropping the camera poses. A forgotten metric flag was not harmful compared with photos only, though the scene came out 16% too big.
6. The model copies a bad hint almost completely, even though its confidence drops. For a single bad camera it took on 96% of the wrong pose (median "absorption" 0.96). Yet its own confidence ranks the bad view lower than the good ones 91% of the time (AUROC 0.91). It has the warning signal but does not use it, which matters for E3.
7. Damage spreads to good views only modestly. One bad camera raised the good views' rotation error by 3-8%; a far jump raised their 3D error by 13%; with 20% of cameras wrong, the good views' rotation error rose 20%, just over the preregistered bar.

Sources: family_summary.csv, dose_response.csv, fix.csv, contamination.csv, absorption.csv, confidence_auroc.csv, scale.csv (reports/e1_results/). "Worse than" always compares the same scenes with and without the hint.
