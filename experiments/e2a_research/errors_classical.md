# E2-A draft: literature-realistic hint-error sizes + classical tools for wrong measurements

Status: DRAFT for E2-A (literature audit). Literature only, no code. All sources below were opened on **2026-10-29** (date read). Every number is quoted from the opened source. Arithmetic conversions are mine and marked "arith". Anything not sourced is marked **UNSOURCED** or **ASSUMPTION**.

Our geometry (from the task brief, not re-checked here): L = median camera distance from the clip centroid, 0.25-6.76 m (median ~1.2 m) over the 10 eval scenes. Clip path 2.8-51.6 m (median ~5.6 m). Consecutive-photo spacing 0.3-4.2 m. HFOV ~85 deg (two scenes 61 deg). Images 518 x 336. View 0 is the reference and is never corrupted.

Conversion conventions used throughout (arith):
- metres to L: x m / L, given at L = 0.25 m / 1.2 m / 6.76 m (smallest / median / largest scene).
- 1 deg of rotation in pixels at image centre: f = 259 / tan(HFOV/2). HFOV 85 deg: f = 259/0.9163 = 282.7 px, so 1 deg ~ 4.9 px. HFOV 61 deg: f = 259/0.5890 = 439.7 px, so 1 deg ~ 7.7 px.
- Our drift family: per-step scale (1+s)^k at step k = 1..9 (so x1.09/1.20/1.42 at step 9). On a straight path with equal spacing d, the end-point translation error is d * sum_k((1+s)^k - 1) = d * [((1+s)^10 - (1+s))/s - 9]: s=1% gives 0.462 d, s=2% gives 0.950 d, s=4% gives 2.006 d; as a fraction of the 9 d path: **5.1% / 10.6% / 22.3%** (heading bias ignored; it adds lateral error on top).
- AbsRel of a pure global depth scale s is |s - 1|: x1.5 gives 0.50, x0.67 gives 0.33. delta1 (pixels within 25%) of a pure global scale is 100% if max(s, 1/s) < 1.25 and 0% otherwise.

## (b) Classical tools for wrong measurements (context notes)

1. **Huber (1964), robust M-estimation of location.** "Robust Estimation of a Location Parameter", Ann. Math. Statist. 35(1): 73-101, DOI 10.1214/aoms/1177703732 (metadata: https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1214/aoms/1177703732; abstract read as OpenAlex inverted-index reconstruction: https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1214/aoms/1177703732; Project Euclid pages returned empty). Opening words: "This paper contains a new approach toward a theory of robust estimation"; it treats location estimation under contaminated normal distributions with estimators between the sample mean and the sample median, asymptotically most robust among translation-invariant estimators. Huber's psi function as restated by Rousseeuw (1984, p. 871, opened): "ψ(u) = min(k, max(u, − k)), where k is some constant, usually around 1.5." Relevance: a Huber-type estimate of the pose scale ẑp is the natural middle ground between MapAnything's mean and the median probe in E2-C4.
2. **Rousseeuw (1984), least median of squares and the breakdown point.** "Least Median of Squares Regression", J. Amer. Statist. Assoc. 79: 871-880, DOI 10.1080/01621459.1984.10477105 (citation from https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/01621459.1984.10477105; text read from a scanned copy, page 1 rendered: https://www.eecs.yorku.ca/course_archive/2010-11/W/6338/lectures/LeastMedianOfSquares.pdf). Quotes: "the sum is replaced by the median of the squared residuals. The resulting estimator can resist the effect of nearly 50% of contamination in the data."; "whereas the breakdown point of the sample median is 50%, it can be shown that L1 regression yields the same value ε* = 0 as LS." Relevance: least squares, and hence the sample mean, has breakdown point 0: one camera moved by 10 L can drag a mean-based ẑp arbitrarily; a median-based ẑp tolerates < 50% bad views. (Arith: with 9 posed non-reference views, one view moved 10 L farther from view 0 raises the mean distance by up to 10L/9 ~ 1.1 L.)
3. **Mangelson, Dominic, Eustice, Vasudevan (ICRA 2018), pairwise consistency maximization (PCM).** "Pairwise Consistent Measurement Set Maximization for Robust Multi-robot Map Merging", ICRA 2018, pp. 2916-2923 (pages from search snippet). PDF: https://robots.engin.umich.edu/publications/jmangelson-2018a.pdf . Quotes: "Existing robust SLAM methods assume a good initialization or an "odometry backbone" to classify inlier and outlier loop closures."; PCM "estimates the largest pairwise internally consistent set of measurements"; it "can be transformed into an instance of the maximum clique problem". Relevance: a template for E3 — keep the largest set of hints that are mutually consistent (and consistent with the images), with no trusted backbone; but pairwise consistency cannot flag errors that are *shared* by all hints (`scale_all`, `shared_heading_bias`, `focal_all`).
4. **Yang, Antonante, Tzoumas, Carlone (RA-L 2020), graduated non-convexity (GNC).** "Graduated Non-Convexity for Robust Spatial Perception: From Non-Minimal Solvers to Global Outlier Rejection", IEEE RA-L 2020, arXiv 1909.08605. https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.08605 . Quotes: GNC can "compute robust solutions, without requiring an initial guess"; solvers "robust to 70-80% of outliers"; "GNC's global optimality cannot be guaranteed". Relevance: robust losses (Geman-McClure/TLS) annealed from convex to non-convex; a candidate for down-weighting bad hints without a clean initial estimate.
5. **Hartley, Trumpf, Dai, Li (IJCV 2013), rotation averaging.** "Rotation Averaging", IJCV 103(3): 267-305, DOI 10.1007/s11263-012-0601-0. https://researchportalplus.anu.edu.au/en/publications/rotation-averaging/ . Quote: "We consider three main problems: single rotation averaging, in which a single rotation is computed" (from several estimates); the others are multiple rotation averaging from relative rotations and conjugate rotation averaging; the paper gives convergence/convexity results, "radius of convergence of the algorithms, and existence of local minima". Relevance: E1 harm is rotation-driven; rotation averaging over image-derived relative rotations is the classical way to check or replace per-view rotation hints. (Robust L1 variants exist in other papers, not verified from this abstract.)
6. **Triggs, McLauchlan, Hartley, Fitzgibbon (2000), robust bundle adjustment.** "Bundle Adjustment — A Modern Synthesis", in Vision Algorithms: Theory and Practice, LNCS 1883, pp. 298-372, DOI 10.1007/3-540-44480-7_21 (citation via OpenAlex). PDF mirror: https://ais-lab.di.unimi.it/Teaching/SIA/Old/IntelligentSystems_2003_2004/Documents/Vision/02_TriggsMcLauchRichard_Bundle%20Adjustment%20-%20A%20Modern%20Synthesis.pdf . Quotes: "The theory is developed for general robust cost functions rather than restricting attention to traditional nonlinear least squares."; "large deviations ("outliers") are much more probable for Cauchy variates than for Gaussian ones"; and, directly relevant to our correlated families: "If all of the observations had been affected about equally (e.g. as by an incorrect projection model), we might still know that something was wrong, but not be able to fix it by simple data cleaning." Relevance: robust per-observation losses fix isolated bad hints; shared errors (wrong focal on all views, wrong global scale) need model-level checks instead.
7. **Julier & Uhlmann (1997), covariance intersection (CI).** "A non-divergent estimation algorithm in the presence of unknown correlations", Proc. American Control Conference 1997, pp. 2369-2373, DOI 10.1109/ACC.1997.609105. Abstract read via OpenAlex reconstruction: https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1109/ACC.1997.609105 . Quotes: "cross-correlation in errors between different random variables are unknown"; CI "yields consistent estimates irrespective of actual correlations". Relevance: fusing a hint with the image-only estimate when their errors may be correlated (e.g. hints that were themselves computed from the same images by SfM/VIO) without becoming over-confident.
